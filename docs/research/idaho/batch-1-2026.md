# H0551 — Taxation, assessment of certain property

House Bill 551 amends Idaho Code § 63-301A, the new construction roll used in property-tax assessment. In plain terms, it revises how taxable new-construction value is counted for fire protection districts and ambulance service districts, including districts that are not subject to urban-renewal revenue-allocation financing. It was introduced by the House Revenue and Taxation Committee, passed the House 68-0-2 and the Senate 31-0-4, and was signed by the Governor on March 11, 2026, as Session Law Chapter 19, effective retroactively to January 1, 2026.

## 1. What it changes
Amends existing Idaho Code § 63-301A, “New construction roll.” It does not add a new section.

In plain words, from the bill text, subsection (3)(k) addresses fire protection districts and ambulance service districts. For a fire protection or ambulance service district that encompasses all or part of an urban renewal revenue allocation area that the district withdrew from under § 50-2906(5), eighty percent of the increment value may be included on the new construction roll for the year of withdrawal. Each year thereafter, ninety percent of the taxable value of otherwise qualifying new construction shall be included on the new construction roll for such a district, and for fire protection and ambulance service districts that are not subject to the financing provisions of an urban renewal revenue allocation area under § 50-2906(4), even if that new construction would not otherwise qualify because it was located within a revenue allocation area.

Section 2 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after passage and approval, and retroactively to January 1, 2026.

# H0573 — Enhanced licenses to carry concealed weapons

House Bill 573 amends Idaho Code § 18-3302K, which governs enhanced concealed-carry licenses. The change visible in the bill text expands who may deliver the required instruction on Idaho firearms law and the use of deadly force, adding certain long-experienced, nationally certified firearms instructors alongside members of the Idaho State Bar and specified law-enforcement officers. It passed the House 68-0-2 and the Senate 33-0-2 and was signed by the Governor on March 26, 2026, as Session Law Chapter 159, effective July 1, 2026.

## 1. What it changes
Amends existing Idaho Code § 18-3302K, “Issuance of enhanced licenses to carry concealed weapons,” and makes a technical correction. It does not add a new section.

In plain words, from the bill text, the required qualifying handgun course must still include instruction in Idaho law relating to firearms and the use of deadly force. That legal instruction must be delivered by any of the following, whose name and credential must appear on the certificate: (A) an active, senior, or emeritus member of the Idaho State Bar; (B) a law enforcement officer, active or retired, who currently possesses or possessed at retirement an intermediate or higher Idaho POST certificate; or (C) an instructor certified by the National Rifle Association, or equivalent, who has instructed an approved course for enhanced concealed carry for at least eight years. In the printed bill, “either” is changed to “any of the following,” and item (C) is added.

Section 2 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.

# H0592 — Hospitals, patient immigration status data collection

House Bill 592 would have added a new Idaho Code § 39-1397 requiring every Medicaid-accepting hospital to ask, on admission or registration forms, whether a patient is a U.S. citizen, lawfully present, or not lawfully present, and to send quarterly counts to the Department of Health and Welfare, which would report annually to the Governor and legislative leaders. The official bill page lists no actions after it was referred to the House Health and Welfare Committee on February 5, 2026. The 2026 regular session ended April 2, 2026. The bill was not enacted, and there were no floor votes.

## 1. What it changes
Would have added a new section, Idaho Code § 39-1397, “Patient immigration status data collection,” to Chapter 13, Title 39. It would not have amended an existing section. The bill did not become law.

In plain words, from the introduced bill text: every hospital that accepts Medicaid would have to include a provision on patient admission or registration forms for the patient or the patient’s representative to state or indicate the patient’s immigration status, including whether the patient is a United States citizen, is lawfully present in the United States, or is not lawfully present. The inquiry would have to be followed by a statement that the response will not affect patient care or result in a report of the patient’s immigration status to immigration authorities.

Every hospital would submit a quarterly report to the Department of Health and Welfare within 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter, including the number of admissions or emergency-department visits in the previous quarter in those three self-reported categories. By March 1 of each year, the department would submit a report to the Governor, the President Pro Tempore of the Senate, and the Speaker of the House, with yearly totals by those categories and information relating to the costs of uncompensated care for aliens not lawfully present, the impact of uncompensated care on hospitals’ cost or ability to provide services, hospital funding needs, and other related information.

The department could promulgate rules, subject to legislative approval, on report format and acceptable form language. The rules could not require disclosure of patient names or other personal identifying information to the department. “Immigration status” is defined by reference to the Immigration and Nationality Act and includes listed groups: citizens; permanent residents (including conditional permanent residents); nonimmigrants; refugees and asylees; and “undocumented migrants or illegal aliens who have overstayed their visa or entered the United States without authorization,” regardless of any temporary status granted by the Department of Homeland Security.

Section 2 declared an emergency and would have made the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.

# H0594 — Taxation, late charges and interest

House Bill 594 amends Idaho Code §§ 63-903, 63-1201, and 63-1202 so that late charges and interest collected with delinquent property taxes are remitted and apportioned to taxing districts in the same proportion as each district receives in property taxes. The Statement of Purpose says the bill codifies a 2025 Idaho Supreme Court decision on that point. It passed the House 69-0-1 and the Senate 31-0-4 and was signed by the Governor on March 12, 2026, as Session Law Chapter 20, effective July 1, 2026.

## 1. What it changes
Amends three existing sections. It does not add a new section.

Idaho Code § 63-903. Adds that all property taxes and fees, together with any costs, late charges, and interest collected by the county tax collector, shall be remitted to the county auditor as provided in § 63-1201, and that the county auditor shall apportion the late charges and interest among the taxing districts in the same proportion as each taxing district receives in property taxes from the total amount collected in property taxes for the entire county.

Idaho Code § 63-1201. Revises the tax collector’s monthly settlement with the county auditor so that it covers all moneys collected, including property taxes levied on property, together with any late charges and interest collected.

Idaho Code § 63-1202. Revises the county auditor’s settlement so that the transmittal to each taxing district includes all moneys belonging to the district, including all property taxes together with late charges and interest, paid into the county treasury since the last transmittal.

# H0599 — Public School Digital Content and Curriculum Fund

House Bill 599 amends Idaho Code § 33-4804 so that money from the Public School Digital Content and Curriculum Fund is distributed to local education agencies under competitive, needs-based criteria set by the State Department of Education, instead of on a first-come, first-served basis. The $50,000 cap on a single request and other qualification rules remain. It passed the House 68-0-2 and the Senate 35-0-0 and was signed by the Governor on March 19, 2026, as Session Law Chapter 60, effective July 1, 2026.

## 1. What it changes
Amends existing Idaho Code § 33-4804, “Public school digital content and curriculum fund,” and makes technical corrections. It does not add a new section.

In plain words, from the bill text: subject to availability, moneys in the fund shall still be distributed at the request of a local education agency (LEA) and shall be based on the amount requested. A single request shall not exceed $50,000. The printed bill strikes the rule that distributions be made “on a first-come, first-served basis” and the rule that a request that cannot be funded for lack of moneys be prioritized “in the order received.” In their place, distributions shall be made to LEAs that meet the subsection’s requirements “based on competitive, needs-based criteria established by the state department of education.” If distributions cannot be made for lack of moneys, requests by eligible LEAs shall be prioritized according to those competitive, needs-based criteria once moneys become available. Two or more LEAs may still jointly request a distribution. Qualification still requires technology infrastructure and an explanation of how the distribution will improve performance and enhance student achievement, including the items already listed in the statute. Additional distributions after an initial award still require that the LEA has met or is making demonstrable progress toward its measurable targets. “Must” is changed to “shall” in two places as a technical correction.

Section 2 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.

# H0612 — Solar, portable solar generators

House Bill 612 would have created a new Idaho Code § 61-122 defining “portable solar generation devices,” exempted them from interconnection and net-metering requirements, barred electric corporations from requiring approval, fees, or extra equipment for such devices, and shielded utilities from liability for damage or injury they cause. It also would have excluded those devices from the definition of “public utility” and from the Residential Solar Energy System Disclosure Act, and would have raised that Act’s capacity threshold from one kilowatt to 1,200 watts. The official bill page lists no actions after referral to House State Affairs on February 11, 2026. The session ended April 2, 2026. The bill was not enacted, and there were no floor votes.

## 1. What it changes
Would have added a new section, Idaho Code § 61-122, and amended §§ 61-129, 48-1802, and 48-1803. The bill did not become law.

New § 61-122 would have defined a “portable solar generation device” as a moveable photovoltaic generation device that is designed to be connected to a building’s electrical system through a standard electrical outlet; is intended to offset part or all of a customer’s electricity consumption; is limited to supplying a maximum power output of not more than 1,200 watts to the utility electric grid; is certified by Underwriters Laboratories or an equivalent nationally recognized testing laboratory; and meets the standards of the most recent National Electrical Code. Notwithstanding any other provision of law, such a device would have been exempt from interconnection and net metering requirements. An electric corporation would not have been allowed to require a customer using such a device to obtain the corporation’s approval before installing or using it, to pay any fee or charge related to the device or for feeding energy back into the grid, or to install any additional controls or equipment beyond what is integrated into the device. The electric corporation would not have been liable for any damage or injury caused by a portable solar generation device.

§ 61-129 would have been amended to state that the term “public utility” shall not include portable solar generation devices, with a related renumbering of existing subsections.

§ 48-1802 would have changed the definition of “residential solar energy system” so that the electricity delivery capacity must exceed 1,200 watts, replacing “one (1) kilowatt.”

§ 48-1803 would have added that the Residential Solar Energy System Disclosure Act does not apply to portable solar generation devices as defined in § 61-122.

“The landmark U.S. Supreme Court decision Plyler v Doe (1982) ruled that public schools cannot deny any student enrollment based on immigration status. In order to more accurately assess the cost of educating children of people having foreign status, the purpose of this bill is to require public schools to collect and report the immigration status and language group of each enrolled student. This statute directs school districts to aggregate the immigration status already collected in student records per Idaho Code 33-133(1 )(j) and 33-133(3)( c) (vi), using categories specified in 8 U.S.C. 1101 and 8 U.S.C. 1641 of the U.S. Immigration and Nationality Act. These data are needed to better inform the state department of education and the Idaho legislature regarding the costs to educate these students.”
