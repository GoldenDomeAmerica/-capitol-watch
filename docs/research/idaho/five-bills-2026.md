# H0623 — Schools, moment of silence

## 1. What it changes
Adds a new section, Idaho Code § 33-1639, “Moment of silence in public schools.” It does not amend an existing section.

In plain words, from the bill text: at or near the start of each school day, in all grades and all Idaho public schools, the teacher or school employee in charge of a classroom must provide at least 60 seconds of silence, with every pupil participating. During that period a student may reflect, meditate, pray, or do another silent activity, as long as the student does not interfere with or distract another student. No other activities may take place during the period. Staff must require pupils to remain silent and make no distracting display. Public-school personnel may not instruct students on the nature of any reflection, other than as the section itself provides. Each public school must notify parents and legal guardians about the period of silence and must encourage them to give pupils guidance on it.

Section 2 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.

# H0559 — Internal revenue code conformity

## 1. What it changes
Amends three existing sections. It does not add a new section.

Idaho Code § 63-3004. Updates the state’s definition of “Internal Revenue Code” from the Code as in effect on January 1, 2025, to the Code as in effect on January 1, 2026. Two exceptions remain: (1) IRC § 85 is applied as in effect on January 1, 2020; and (2) specified research or experimental expenditures, as defined in IRC § 174 as in effect on December 31, 2024, incurred in taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2022, and before January 1, 2025, continue to be treated under the Internal Revenue Code provisions in effect immediately before enactment of section 70302 of P.L. 119-21, rather than under that section.

Idaho Code § 63-3022O. Extends Idaho’s existing add-back / decoupling rules for federal bonus depreciation so they also cover IRC § 168(n), not only § 168(k). In the printed bill, “subsection (k)” is changed to “subsections (k) and (n)” wherever those depreciation adjustments are described, including basis, later-year add-backs, pass-through records, and the extended assessment and refund period for those items.

Idaho Code § 63-3029G. Keeps the existing Idaho credit for increasing research activities in Idaho, and adds that amounts deducted or amortized under IRC § 174 or § 174A that constitute qualified research expenses, qualified research, basic research payments, or basic research, as defined in this section, are not eligible for that credit.

Section 4 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after passage and approval, and retroactive to January 1, 2025.

# H0708 — Homeowner’s associations, dissolve

## 1. What it changes
Would have added a new section, Idaho Code § 55-3204C, “Automatic dissolution of homeowner’s associations,” to Chapter 32, Title 55 (the Homeowner’s Association Act). It would not have amended an existing section. The bill did not become law.

In plain words, from the introduced bill text: any homeowner’s association that, as of July 1, 2026, had existed for 10 years or more, measured from the filing date of its articles of incorporation, would automatically dissolve on July 1, 2029, unless its members voted against dissolution under this section. Any association in existence for less than 10 years as of July 1, 2026, or created after that date, would automatically dissolve 10 years after its articles were filed, unless members voted against dissolution.

Before those dates, an association would have to hold a membership meeting so members could vote against automatic dissolution, with notice under Idaho Code § 30-30-505 stating that a purpose of the meeting was to consider automatic dissolution, and with a copy or summary of a plan of dissolution. Automatic dissolution would not occur if a majority of the eligible voters voted against it.

Unless the declaration or governing documents provided otherwise, common property (including common areas, limited common areas, other property or improvements owned or maintained by the association, or property owned in common by the members) would remain dedicated to the members’ common use. The plan of dissolution would have to provide for disposition of the association’s assets by transfer to a successor entity, the lot owners, or a local government.

If members did not vote against automatic dissolution, the association would dissolve under the plan. If they did vote against it, the association would continue under the same terms, then automatically dissolve 10 years after that first successful vote against dissolution, and every 10 years after that, unless and until members failed to vote against dissolution.

# H0736 — Elections, ballots

## 1. What it changes
Amends existing Idaho Code § 34-903, which directs the Secretary of State to prescribe the form and contents of ballots and related documents.

In plain words, from the bill text, the substantive change is in subsection (4). Previously, candidate-name rotation determined by the Secretary of State applied to federal, state, county, and city offices in a political entity whose registered-voter count at the last general election exceeded 25,000, and a separate rule required rotation for “other offices” only if the entity’s registered-voter count exceeded 100,000. The bill strikes “city” and inserts “local,” so rotation under the 25,000-voter rule applies to federal, state, county, and local offices. It deletes the separate 100,000-voter rule for other offices, and renumbers the remaining provision: in all other elections, name order is determined by applying the first letter of each candidate’s last name to a random alphabet selected before each election by the Secretary of State.

The rest of § 34-903 (the Secretary of State’s duty to prescribe forms, statewide uniformity, printing legislative or special-district names only on those districts’ ballots, and the limit on appearing for more than one partisan or judicial office) is otherwise left in place, with a minor punctuation change in subsection (2)(a).

Section 2 declares an emergency and makes the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.

The current statute page for § 34-903 matches that enacted language and cites “am. 2026, ch. 186, sec. 1.”

# S1349 — Machine guns

## 1. What it changes
Would have added two new sections to Chapter 33, Title 18, Idaho Code, plus legislative findings, a severability clause, and an effective date. It would not have amended an existing section. The bill did not become law.

Section 1 (findings and intent), from the bill text, states that federal law currently restricts manufacture, transfer, and possession of certain machine guns under 18 U.S.C. 922(o), and that if those restrictions are repealed, invalidated, or otherwise rendered unenforceable, Idaho should immediately recognize and protect the right of law-abiding citizens and Idaho-based industry to lawfully possess, manufacture, transfer, and sell machine guns in the state, subject only to otherwise applicable state law. It describes a contingent, or “trigger,” framework and a prohibition on using state and local resources to enforce federal restrictions that are no longer valid or enforceable.

New § 18-3328, “Machine guns — contingent authorization,” would define “machine gun” as any firearm that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. A “trigger event” would be the first of: (i) congressional repeal of 18 U.S.C. 922(o) or any successor provision restricting civilian possession, transfer, or manufacture of machine guns; (ii) a U.S. Supreme Court decision holding that provision unconstitutional in whole or in relevant part; (iii) a decision of a federal court of competent jurisdiction that is binding in Idaho, that permanently enjoins enforcement of that provision in Idaho, and that is not stayed; or (iv) formal written notice by the Idaho Attorney General that ATF, or a successor agency, has ceased enforcing that provision as to law-abiding civilians generally.

Upon a trigger event, and subject to subsection (3), it would be lawful under Idaho law for any person not otherwise prohibited from possessing firearms under state or federal law to possess, purchase, receive, sell, transfer, or manufacture a machine gun in this state. No state agency, political subdivision, or employee could adopt or enforce a rule, regulation, ordinance, or policy with the purpose or effect of prohibiting or unduly burdening that lawful activity. The section would not authorize possession by a person prohibited from possessing firearms, would not affect generally applicable criminal laws (including use of firearms in a crime, reckless endangerment, or unlawful discharge), and would not require any person or entity to manufacture, sell, or transfer a machine gun. Within 30 days after determining that a trigger event had occurred, the Attorney General would have to publish notice on the official website and transmit it to the Governor, the President Pro Tempore of the Senate, the Speaker of the House, and the Director of the Idaho State Police. Subsections (2) and (3) would become operative on the 30th day after that notice.

New § 18-3329, “Prohibition on enforcement of invalid federal restrictions,” would provide that, upon a trigger event, no Idaho agency, political subdivision, or employee acting in an official capacity could expend funds or use personnel or other resources to enforce, or assist in enforcing, 18 U.S.C. 922(o) or a successor civilian machine-gun restriction, to the extent it had been repealed, invalidated, or rendered unenforceable as described in § 18-3328; or provide material support or resources to a federal agency or official for investigating, citing, or prosecuting a person for conduct that is lawful under § 18-3328. It would not prohibit cooperation or information-sharing about conduct that remains unlawful under state or federal law, and would not limit state or local law enforcement’s authority to investigate or prosecute violations of state law.

Section 4 is a severability clause. Section 5 declares an emergency and would have made the act effective on and after July 1, 2026.
